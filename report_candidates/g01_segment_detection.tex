\subsection{Segment-level detection}
\label{sec:g01-segments}

The frozen verdict is one clip-level median, so a violation that covers part of a clip is
averaged away. We slide an 8-frame window (0.66\,s at the 12\,Hz clip rate; hop 4 frames) over
each clip, compute the clip-level tiers on every window, and calibrate the maximum over windows
conformally with leave-one-flight-out folds. Calibrating the maximum, rather than a single
window, gives each clip one family-wise false-reject budget; the window length is the
label-free rule's \mbox{$0.5/f_{95}$} result (0.05--0.22\,s at the truth rate) with its
8-sample floor applied at the rate the statistic is computed on, because 0.6--2.6 frames
support no statistic at all.

\paragraph{The estimator's tail, not the physics, sets the threshold.} With the ungated
calibration the conformal 95th percentile of the clip maximum is 89--94\,deg: four clips of
\texttt{flight-13a-trackRATM} (and one of \texttt{07a-lemniscate}) carry a single window above
89\,deg, and every fold containing those flights inherits it. Detection then equals the
false-reject rate for every edit family, and the residual tier's flags for a $0.5\times$ edit,
a $1.5\times$ edit, the yaw control and the null edit are identical (7 of 82 each). The frozen
S3 confidence gate --- label-free, per clip, already part of the verdict --- drops 28 of the
100 real clips and every clip whose maximum exceeds 80\,deg, so we calibrate on the gate-kept
population and report the ungated block in full beside it.

\paragraph{Result.} At a matched false-reject the scan statistic detects partial violations
markedly better than the whole-clip median: 2.5$\times$ at 25\,\% coverage and 1.7$\times$ at
50\,\% for tilt removal and mirrored roll (Table~\ref{tab:g01-detection}), with the yaw and null
controls at the false-reject rate. It does not meet the pre-registered bar (half the clips at
25\,\% and 50\,\% coverage, interval IoU above 0.5): IoU is 0.15--0.24 and localisation is
seconds-scale, because a flagged window necessarily over-covers the edit and 1.3--2.0 false
segments per real minute survive on real clips. Scale edits are not this machinery's target: a
$x1.5$ edit is caught by the whole-clip median in 48 of 69 clips and by the window residual in 7.

\paragraph{What this does not show.} Segments require a path dense enough to differentiate at
the truth rate; ANWM's 1\,Hz, 16-point paths cannot support the statistic and no generated clip
is scored here. The two held-out flights (frozen before scoring) turn out to hold three
gate-kept clips, and the dev-fitted thresholds do not transfer to them: the held-out false-reject
is 2/3 with the null edit flagged as often as an edit. That is a warning about per-flight
calibration spread, not a rate, and it is reported rather than repaired.
