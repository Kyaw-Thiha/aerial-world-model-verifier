\section{Segment detection on the simulated set}
\label{sec:g3-segments}

The frozen verdict is one clip-level median, so a violation that covers part of a flight is
averaged away. Earlier work found that a sliding window scan finds partial violations but
localises them poorly (mean IoU 0.15--0.24 on real clips), while the scale-free coherence tier
was the only tier a per-clip nuisance fit could not absorb and had the smallest detectable local
step (16.8$^\circ$ at 25\,\% coverage). The simulated labelled set (300 clips, exact violation
intervals, 30\,fps, exact required lean) makes it possible to ask which of false-discovery-rate
control, boundary refinement, per-clip time alignment and multi-scale windows actually improve
localisation, and what the smallest localisable violation is.

\paragraph{Population and reader.}
170 development clips over three procedural scene families and four camera mounts; 80 feasible,
the rest whole-clip or partial camera defects plus one path-side thrust-limit clip. Every clip
was read with GeoCalib (pinhole, focal prior) at every video frame. The reader is stochastic run
to run: two identical runs differ by a median of $2.2\times10^{-3}$ lean units (p99
$2.2\times10^{-2}$), and rare single frames jump by up to a few degrees. This envelope is quoted
wherever the reader enters a number.

\paragraph{Window statistic.}
The primary arm is a 12-frame (0.40\,s) window, hop 4, with the coherence statistic
$1-\min(r_x,r_y)$ and the clip decision taken as the maximum over windows against a
leave-one-scene-out conformal 95th percentile. The comparison is the 8-frame residual scan; the
whole-clip median is the baseline. Nothing is fitted per clip.

\paragraph{What limits the window tier.}
Two limits appear before any detection rate is quoted, and both are properties of the design
rather than of the violation. First, the coherence statistic needs both axes of both leans to
vary inside a window; on steady turns, constant-speed ellipses, straight accelerations and
climbs one axis is constant, so the tier abstains. On the simulated set the tier is testable on
the manoeuvres whose lean direction rotates (slalom, lemniscate) and not on the others. Second,
and more sharply, a level camera has almost no lean variance, so \emph{tilt removal makes the
coherence tier abstain rather than fire}: the real-corpus detection of tilt removal came from
the reader's own $\sim4^\circ$ noise supplying that variance, which a low-noise simulated reader
does not provide.

\paragraph{Calibration.}
Calibrating the maximum over roughly one hundred windows per clip saturates the conformal
threshold: the coherence threshold sits at 1.94 of a maximum 2.0, and the residual threshold at
60--80$^\circ$, carried by three feasible clips with catastrophic reader windows. Benjamini--
Hochberg within a clip is the pre-registered response; it restores a usable calibration because
it works in $p$-space rather than on an extreme order statistic.
