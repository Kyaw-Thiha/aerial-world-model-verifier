\subsection{What limits the verifier: the reader's error, measured}
\label{sec:j-reader-error}

Two rounds of segment work and the published detection floors all pointed at the same open number:
how accurately the tilt is read from pixels, and how much calibration slack sits around it. This
section measures that number instead of assuming it. Per-frame reader output against mocap is
taken on the frames the verdict scores (5{,}007 frames, eight TII flights, \SI{12}{Hz}), for the
frozen GeoCalib reader and the leave-one-flight-out fine-tuned reader of G03, and the error is
split into a per-flight constant, drift below \SI{0.5}{Hz} and everything above it with an
orthogonal DCT split, so that the three shares are exactly additive. The estimate is log-mapped
onto the lean tangent basis at the truth, so the two axis errors satisfy
$\sqrt{e_{\mathrm{pitch}}^2+e_{\mathrm{roll}}^2}=\theta$ to machine precision and the axes are an
orthogonal decomposition of the published scalar error, not a second quantity. The pipeline
reproduces the published numbers before adding to them: the tail fraction is
$638/5{,}007=\SI{12.75}{\percent}$ against \SI{12.7}{\percent} in report~02, and the per-flight
tail fractions match G03 frame for frame.

\paragraph{The error is not one thing.} Pooled over the eight flights and both axes the frozen
reader's squared error is \SI{29.6}{\percent} per-flight constant, \SI{40.4}{\percent} drift below
\SI{0.5}{Hz} and \SI{30.0}{\percent} above it (flight-clustered bootstrap, 5{,}000 replicates,
eight clusters: a descriptive spread, not a confidence interval, and the interval on the bias share
is wide, \numrange{12.4}{41.7}). The pooled number is three flights' opinion: the three racing
flights carry \SI{83}{\percent} of the corpus's squared error. On the five non-racing flights the
picture inverts --- \texttt{01p} and \texttt{07p} are \SI{68}{\percent} and \SI{72}{\percent}
constant on the pitch axis at a \SI{2.7}{deg} RMS. On the clips the frozen confidence gate actually
keeps the bias share rises to \SI{42.8}{\percent} and the fast share falls to
\SI{19.8}{\percent}; on all frames at once the three racing flights dominate and the shares are
\SI{29.6}{/}\SI{40.4}{/}\SI{30.0}{\percent}. The pre-registered rule that would have opened the
per-clip-calibration branch (bias share $\ge \SI{40}{\percent}$ of the trimmed variance) does not
fire as written (\SI{35.2}{\percent}, and the trimmed split is not even identified: trimming on
the error selects on the sum of the components and leaves a closure of \SI{-89}{\percent} of the
total). The stratum conflict is reported, not resolved.

\paragraph{The bias is a forward pitch offset.} Pitch bias is positive on eight of eight flights
and pinned to \SIrange{2.15}{2.69}{deg} with standard errors of \SIrange{0.03}{0.18}{deg} on the
five non-racing flights; roll bias is \SIrange{-0.52}{1.43}{deg} and changes sign
(Table~\ref{tab:j-bias}). This is the direct measurement of the \SIrange{2}{5}{deg} per-flight bias
G2 inferred from quiet windows. It is not one rig-level mount rotation: a rotation fitted
leave-one-flight-out moves the pooled median error from \SI{3.90}{deg} to \SI{3.74}{deg} and makes
the three track flights \emph{worse} (\num{3.60} to \num{4.14}, \num{7.75} to \num{8.32},
\num{7.32} to \num{8.49}). A per-flight constant removes \SIrange{1}{1.5}{deg} of median error on
four of the five non-racing flights, which is what the verdict's $\pm\SI{7}{deg}$ per-clip uptilt
nuisance already does. Within a flight the only consistent covariate is heading: the roll error
moves with yaw at $t\approx2.4$ in both readers.

\paragraph{The error correlates on a sub-second to five-second scale.} The integral correlation
time of the scalar error is \SI{0.22}{}, \SI{0.77}{}, \SI{0.26}{}, \SI{3.17}{}, \SI{0.86}{},
\SI{5.23}{}, \SI{0.34}{} and \SI{0.29}{s} per flight (median \SI{0.56}{s}). For an error that is
one third fast, the ``fast'' band is \SIrange{0.5}{6}{Hz}, not per-frame white noise: a
complementary filter that averages sub-second windows buys much less than $\sqrt{n}$.

\paragraph{The tail is a flight property, and the gate already removes it.} \SI{12.75}{\percent}
of frames exceed \SI{10}{deg}, concentrated on the three racing flights (\SI{33.6}{},
\SI{35.4}{} and \SI{34.6}{\percent}) and almost absent on the two quiet piloted ones
(\SI{0.8}{} and \SI{0.5}{\percent}). \num{558} of the \num{638} tail frames sit in clips the
frozen S3 gate drops; \num{80} survive. Only \SI{21.6}{\percent} of tail frames are in short
($\le3$-frame) runs and \SI{39.5}{\percent} are in runs of a second or more, of which 9 of 12 are
identifiable from tilt (8/8 flights), speed (6/8), the estimator's own confidence (5/8) or a
Laplacian-variance blur proxy (7/8).

\paragraph{Fusion has no usable rotation source on TII.} Measured as the error of carrying the
true gravity forward with the estimated rotations, which is what a filter consumes: on the UZH
indoor forward sequences DPVO and ViPE give \SIrange{0.06}{0.14}{deg} per \SI{1/15}{s} step and
\SIrange{0.19}{0.29}{deg} after one second, twenty times inside the bar of
\SI{0.3}{deg} per frame and \SI{1.5}{deg} per second. On TII, DPVO gives \SI{2.80}{deg} per step
and \SI{7.76}{deg} after one second, and on \texttt{16p} its estimated relative rotation is
\SI{1.5}{\percent} of the true rotation budget --- the rotation is close to frozen. The
innovation-gated fusion of reader and rotation was therefore not built.

\paragraph{The noise budget, and the target it sets.} Holding every real clip's path and replacing
the depicted side by a perfect depiction plus a controlled error with the measured bias, split and
correlation time gives the minimum detectable uniform tilt reduction at a \SI{5}{\percent}
leave-one-flight-out false-reject (Table~\ref{tab:j-budget}). At the measured accuracy it is
\SI{6.9}{deg} for the whole-clip median and \SI{10.5}{deg} for the eight-frame window the segment
detector uses; with the reader's actual measured error the clean-path floor is \SI{10.7}{deg}; and
the handoff's own construction (the mocap attitude as the perfect depiction, which carries the real
mismatch between the attitude flown and the attitude a drag-free path demands) reproduces the
frozen \SI{14.73}{deg} threshold at \SI{14.46}{deg} with a leave-one-flight-out spread of
\numrange{13.21}{15.51}. Accuracy is the lever ($\sigma=3$, \num{4.8} and \SI{8}{deg} give
\num{4.7}, \num{6.7} and \SI{9.6}{deg}); a \SI{5}{deg} per-flight bias costs about
\SI{1.5}{deg}; the correlation time is second order across the range the generator realises
(\SIrange{0.30}{0.73}{s}). To put the whole-clip floor at \SI{5}{deg} the trimmed RMSE has to fall
from \num{4.8} to about \num{3} --- a further \SI{40}{\percent} on top of G03's \num{3.71}.

\paragraph{Learned confidence ranks the tail and does not decide it.} A logistic model on the
reader's own stored per-frame outputs (44 features, leave-one-flight-out) reaches a per-frame
AUROC of \num{0.946} [\num{0.882}, \num{0.973}] against \num{0.851} for the same model fitted on
the frozen S3 signal alone and \num{0.866} for tilt and speed alone; at clip level, \num{0.961} on
114 clips against the gate's published \num{0.946}. The gain is real and concentrated where S3 is
uninformative (\texttt{04a} \num{0.95} vs \num{0.38}, \texttt{07a} \num{0.97} vs \num{0.55}). It is
not calibrated: the held-out recalibration slope is \num{0.55} with intercept \num{-1.87}, and the
top decile predicts \num{0.96} where \num{0.71} is observed. Downstream it changes nothing ---
weighting the window median by \num{1-p}, masking high-$p$ frames, or both leaves every detection
cell at or below the unweighted statistic at the same false-reject. A robust median over a window
is already doing the job a monotone reweighting of the same information would do.


\paragraph{Per-clip calibration absorbs the violations it is meant to catch.} The bias is real, so
the obvious fix is to fit it out: a free two-vector offset per clip, with the drag coefficient
bounded to $[0,0.40]$ (the handoff's allowance), no gain fitted in any arm and nothing fitted inside
a window, calibrated leave-one-flight-out at \SI{5}{\percent} on the gate-kept population. Table
\ref{tab:j-absorption} is the price. On the clip-median tier the fit removes
\SI{44.9}{} percentage points of full-clip tilt-removal detection and \SI{63.8}{} of the $x1.5$
edit, because a uniform tilt change moves the depicted lean in exactly the direction an offset can
follow and a magnitude change in exactly the direction a drag term can follow; only the mirror
survives in part (\SI{27.5}{} lost), because a sign flip is not a translation. The bounded drag
pins to its upper bound on 65 of the 72 real clips, so it is a second magnitude absorber rather
than a drag estimate. On the window tier the relative residual is break-even for partial edits
(\num{14}/69 against \num{10}/69 at 25\,\% removal) and still loses \num{14} clips on full removal.

The fit-free tier is the one to keep. Per-axis correlation is invariant to a translation and to a
scaling, so neither an offset nor a gain can hide a violation from it; at a \num{3}/69 false-reject
it detects \num{33}/\num{45}/\num{54} of partial-25, partial-50 and full tilt removal and
\num{40}/\num{54}/\num{55} of mirrored roll, and its minimum detectable local step is
\SIrange{14}{17}{deg} against more than \SI{25}{deg} for every fitted statistic. It is not free: its
yaw control is \num{7}-\num{9}/69 against the same \num{3}/69, the view dependence measured above and
already recorded for the gain tier in G02. The design consequence is that a per-clip offset may be
used to \emph{localise}, never to \emph{adjudicate}, and that the coherence tier rather than the
absolute residual is the statistic that survives a nuisance fit.

\paragraph{Consequence for the design.} The measurable headroom is in the tail's admission
decision, not in the fast component: on the flights where the reader works the error is a
\SI{2.4}{deg} pitch offset plus a \SI{1.5}{deg} wobble correlated over half a second, and the
verdict's per-clip mount fit already removes the first part; on the racing flights the error is
\SIrange{17}{32}{deg} RMS with a five-second correlation time and \SI{88}{\percent} of the tail is
already excluded by the gate. A per-clip calibration is worth \SIrange{1}{1.5}{deg} of median error
on the non-racing flights and nothing on the racing ones --- and it must not be the only tier,
because fitting it out costs \SIrange{28}{64}{\percent} of the detection of the edits that matter;
a complementary filter needs a rotation source TII does not have; and a learned confidence, however
well it ranks, does not move a robust window statistic. Of everything tried here, the fit-free
coherence tier on a window is the only statistic that cannot be absorbed and that resolves a local
violation at a usable size.
