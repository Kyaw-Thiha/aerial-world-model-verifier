\section{A simulated labelled flight set}
\label{sec:i01-simset}

Every detection number in this report so far rests on real flights with synthetic edits: we
know the edit we applied, not the physical violation a model committed. That is enough to
validate a statistic, not enough to answer a reviewer who asks for a labelled set of feasible
and infeasible flights with exact ground truth. A simulator supplies it: since a quadrotor
must thrust along its body $z$ axis, a path fixes the attitude it requires
($\zb = \unit(\va + g\ve_3 + k_d \vv)$), so a rendered flight can be made infeasible
\emph{by construction} --- hold the camera level, mirror the roll, scale the tilt --- with the
violation's type, size and exact interval known.

\paragraph{What a simulator has to provide.} Because the required tilt is a closed-form
function of the path and the depicted tilt follows from the camera pose the renderer was
given, the simulator's job is \emph{rendering}, not dynamics: it must draw a known camera
trajectory in a textured 3D scene, headlessly, on a GPU we can rent. Its own flight dynamics
would insert an integrated attitude between the two sides of the comparison and are
deliberately unused.

\paragraph{Candidate comparison.} Four candidates were assessed and three were executed:
Blender with the project's own dynamics, Isaac Sim, Flightmare, and the Unreal-based
AirSim/Colosseum pair. \Cref{tab:i01-candidates} states what was measured and what was only
inspected. Blender is recommended: it renders headless on an L40S with no RT-core requirement,
its pose$\to$pixel model matches its own camera to within half a pixel, and its scene and
flight are both functions of a seed. Its cost is what decides the renderer setting
(\Cref{tab:i01-throughput}): at 640$\times$360 the 180{,}000-frame set costs about USD~18 with
EEVEE and about USD~81 with Cycles, so EEVEE is the default and Cycles the validation subset.
Isaac Sim started headless on the same GPU and is the photorealistic fallback, at the cost of a
10-minute, 15\,GB image and a Python API that moved between releases; no frame was obtained
from it within the spike's time box. Flightmare's dependency set is Ubuntu 18.04 with ROS
Melodic and its renderer is a 2020 Unity binary; Colosseum is archived and AirSim's last
release predates the current ROS and Ubuntu generations.

\paragraph{The read-out that matters first.} The tilt reader was developed on real images, so
its error on simulated frames has to be reported before any detection rate. On a 3\,s slalom
(required tilt 14.6--32.2$^\circ$, truth at 250\,Hz, 91 frames at 30\,fps) the reader's
up-vector error against exact truth is 1.14$^\circ$ median (p90 1.53) on the physically
attitude-rendered clip and 0.36$^\circ$ (p90 0.71) on the level one;
\Cref{tab:i01-reader} gives the depicted lean and the gain slope behind it. The reader is more
accurate on the renders than on real TII frames (3.5--4$^\circ$), as expected in the absence
of motion blur and rolling shutter, so the sim-to-real gap will be dominated by the look of
the scene rather than by the geometry.

\paragraph{Scope.} One flight and two defect families demonstrate the pipeline end to end;
they do not measure detection rates, and no segment-level number exists until the
segment-level detector of \Cref{sec:segments} is applied to the labels.
