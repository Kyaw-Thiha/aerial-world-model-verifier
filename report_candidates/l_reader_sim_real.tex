\section{Reader error on simulated scenes, and what fine-tuning on them transfers}
\label{sec:l-reader-sim}

The verifier's decision layer is physics, but its input is a reader: GeoCalib on the raw fisheye.
Two properties of that reader set the operating point. Its error is scene-dependent --- the
simulated forest reads 7--10.7$^\circ$ at every camera mount while the open families read
0.5--2.8$^\circ$ (Table~\ref{tab:l-grid}) --- and its real bias is a forward pitch offset of
+2.2 to +2.7$^\circ$ that no single rig rotation removes (Track J). This section asks whether
training the reader on simulated scenes with exact attitude fixes either property on real,
held-out flights, and where the transfer fails.

\paragraph{The simulated set.} Track I renders flights from closed-form dynamics, so the camera
attitude is exact: 80 feasible development clips over three scene families and four mounts
(0/15/30/45$^\circ$), 37{,}401 frames, labels built from the render pose and verified against the
published truth to $6\times10^{-12}$$^\circ$. Every clip's video and truth were re-hashed against
the manifest before scoring (160 objects, no mismatch). The four held-out scenes are not scored:
the Track I render was incomplete when this track started, so no held-out-scene number is quoted.

\paragraph{The gate does not remove scene bias.} The frozen S3 confidence gate drops 14 of 26
forest clips but keeps 12 whose median error is 9.96$^\circ$, against 7.59$^\circ$ for the ones
it drops; on the open families it keeps 51 of 54. A verdict that relies on the gate to protect
itself from a scene it has not seen will not be protected.

\paragraph{The error's composition moves with the scene.} With Track J's exact decomposition the
simulated reader splits 23.6\,\% constant / 41.1\,\% slow / 35.3\,\% fast, close to the real TII
totals (29.6/40.4/30.0). But the forest is bias-dominated (40.5/37.4/22.1, almost exactly J's
\emph{gate-kept} real anatomy) while the open scenes are fast-noise-dominated (urban
4.8/44.0/51.2). A per-clip constant is the right correction in one scene and useless in another,
which is why the fine-tune is judged per family and not pooled.

\paragraph{The fine-tune.} Three arms with G03's architecture and a budget matched by 600 gradient
steps per epoch (two epochs): real-only (the G03 recipe), simulated-only, and mixed with domain
randomisation on the simulated side (photometric jitter, camera roll, and a synthetic
simple-divisional warp whose label is exact because a change of camera model leaves the gravity
direction invariant). Folds are leave-one-flight-out over the six \emph{development} real flights
and leave-one-family-out on the simulated side; the two real held-out flights (13p, 07a) are in no
training pool, normalisation or gate fit, and are scored once by the final models. The simulated
held-out scenes are deferred with the data, so the simulated criterion is the out-of-family bias
spread on the development scenes.

\paragraph{What transfers.} On the two held-out real flights the pooled 90\,\%-trimmed per-frame
RMSE is \LTwoRealA{} for the real-only arm, \LTwoRealB{} for the simulated-only arm and
\LTwoRealC{} for the mixed arm, against 8.29$^\circ$ for the G03 fine-tune and 14.75$^\circ$ for
the frozen reader on the same 906 frames. The scene-family bias spread on the out-of-family
simulated predictions is not applicable to the real-only arm and \LTwoSpreadB{} and \LTwoSpreadC{}
for the simulated-only and mixed arms, against 2.26$^\circ$ for the frozen reader --- met by both,
but by levelling the open families from 0.9-1.3$^\circ$ to 2-3.3$^\circ$. Downstream, on the gate-kept real clips the coherence tier's false-reject is
\LTwoDownA{}, \LTwoDownB{} and \LTwoDownC{} against 5/72 for the frozen reader: no arm meets the
pre-registered "does not rise" criterion, and the simulated-only reader collapses full-removal
detection from 70/72 to 43/72 while the mixed arm keeps it at 71/72.

\paragraph{Where it fails.} The failure is concentrated on the ceiling-view racing flight. On
13p the frozen reader's trimmed RMSE is 18.4$^\circ$, G03 brings it to 9.9, real-only to 8.9, and
both simulated arms are worse than the frozen reader (25.7 and 27.1); on 07a the mixed arm is the
best reader measured (median 1.02$^\circ$) but its tail is slightly worse than G03's. The
sim-to-real gap is therefore not a uniform offset: a reader trained only on simulated scenes reads
the easy flight at 6.5$^\circ$ (frozen 6.5) and the hard flight at 25.7 (18.4). The simulated
scene spread is reduced by making every family mediocre, not by improving the hard one, and the
mixed arm's objective did not converge (training loss rose in its second epoch). Under this data,
budget and DR recipe, simulated supervision does not buy real robustness; it buys a flatter
simulated error profile at the cost of the real tail.
